File type: .csv
Description: A survey was performed to collate the available data on the levels of individual haloacetic acids (HAAs) in treated or not treated UK drinking water sources. This data was obtained from UK water companies who reported the levels obtained and in some cases gave information about the type of water to which the HAA data related. Version 1 created 12/11/2025. Version 2 created 07/04/2026.
HAA9 refers to all nine chlorinated and brominated HAAs.
The date relates to the date this version of the data was created.

Information on data classifications and abbreviations used:
Data values were classified in terms of the source water types, treatment type, treatment stage and disinfection process.
Where this information was not provided an Unknown category was assigned.
Source waters were classified as groundwaters (GW) (fed by boreholes, wells, and springs), surface waters (SW) (from rivers and reservoirs) and mixed waters (MX = GW + SW). Further classification was assigned by catchment type as either upland (UL) or lowland (LL) waters to reflect known difference in the organic make up and background water characteristics.
The data provided covered a range of organic removal technologies and was labelled accordingly as either coagulation, ion exchange, nanofiltration (NF) membrane, or minimal treatment.
In addition, different disinfection approaches were covered and sub-categorised based on where chlorination was used: Chlorine (Cl Cl – both primary and residual disinfection), Chlorine/chloramine (Cl ClAm – primary disinfection by chlorination – followed by stabilisation to chloramine), UV/chlorine (UV Cl – residual disinfection by chlorination) and Membrane/chlorine (NF Cl – residual disinfection by chlorination).
The data also covered several stages within the water treatment process and, to ensure comparability, sub-categories were created to isolate different formation patterns:
Raw – untreated source water, prior to treatment or chlorination
Raw Formation Potential (FP) – raw water where the potential for formation of DBPs is assessed by adding chlorine in laboratory conditions, generally with a significant excess of chlorine to assess the potential maximum DBP formation
Water Treatment Works (WTW) Internal – samples from within the treatment plant, at different stages within the process, which had been exposed to chlorine
WTW Miscellaneous – samples where the treatment stage within the WTW is unclear, and includes pilot plants and investigations, and as such is unlikely to be representative
Treated Water – combined from WTW final, distribution and tap:
WTW Final – samples from the outlet of the plant
Distribution – samples from the distribution zone, including storage reservoirs or tanks
Tap – samples taken at customer tap
