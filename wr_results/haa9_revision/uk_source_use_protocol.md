# UK source feasibility and permitted use

Recorded 19 September 2026 after inspecting the released schema and README, before calculating derived group-level chemical accounting. This is a retrospective source-use decision, not a preregistration or untouched-data claim.

The requested external validation requires individual matched chemistry, repeated observations, group identifiers and collection dates. The Cranfield DOI 10.57996/cran.ceres-2807 release provides only overlapping group means. Therefore it will not be used to estimate capture, a sample-budget advantage, a threshold-discordance rate, uncertainty intervals, or a prospective prediction result. Group labels do not identify independent systems.

The only permitted derived quantities are row-wise sums of published species means into HAA5, the additional four species, and HAA9, plus the ratio of the additional-species sum to the nine-species sum. This last quantity is explicitly a ratio of means, not the average of individual-sample ratios. There will be no averaging across overlapping rows or inference from these summaries. All rows, including raw and formation-potential categories, remain in the accounting output, with their original labels preserved. These are contextual chemistry data, not validation of the study's monitoring-policy estimand.

The raw directory contains the source-reader extraction, not an original-byte download. The reader replaced a micro sign in CSV headers and transformed some README formatting. Units are established from the README, and numeric source cells are not altered. The dataset's version date is not a sample date. The README reports collection from January 2020 to May 2025 but releases no row-level collection dates.

## Historical repeated-sample source

An additional official source, DWI70/2/253 (WRc, May 2011), was located after the recent-release feasibility check. Tables 6.2 to 6.5 provide repeated nine-species chemistry for anonymized sites A to T. This amendment was recorded after reading and digitizing the tables, and before running the feasibility audit. Values below the common 60 micrograms per litre benchmark were already visible; this is explicitly an outcome-aware feasibility audit, not a preregistered validation.

Preserve all 80 scheduled site-round slots, including not-sampled entries, and the published censoring flags. Derive coherent species-wise lower and upper concentration bounds: each censored component contributes zero to the lower bound and its stated limit to the upper bound. Retain the published HAA9 total separately because rounding can differ from the sum of rounded components. Compute collection-to-analysis days from tabulated dates, rather than correcting source dates to match narrative ranges. A 14-day method-557 holding-time screen is a necessary but insufficient comparability check; it does not establish that cold-chain or all analytical-quality requirements were met.

Use the same B60 threshold as the primary US analysis and a contextual 80 micrograms per litre sensitivity only. Do not search lower thresholds to manufacture reference-positive samples. Estimate capture only if reference-positive observations exist. If no reference-positive observation exists, report capture, sensitivity, and HAA9-versus-HAA5 advantage as undefined, not zero or perfect. Do not pool the UK study with UCMR4, conduct a system-bootstrap of 20 selected sites, or claim prospective validation. Report site linkage and repeated-visit feasibility, censoring, sampling dates, holding times, missing rounds, and benchmark support. The source's first round is excluded from nine-species accounting because it used GC-microECD and supplied no IC-MS/MS survey samples; the four subsequent rounds use IC-MS/MS based on EPA 557. The report itself discusses the 14-day limit but also reports stability tests up to 68 days. Thus the 14-day comparability screen is not a declaration that delayed observations are invalid.
